# Sponsoring

StackVo is free, MIT-licensed and maintained by one person, with no company
behind it and no paid tier. Nothing is gated, and nothing is going to be.
Sponsoring does not buy a feature; it buys the maintainer's time, and time
is the one thing the project is short of.

## What it pays for

- **Time.** Issues read the day they arrive, and releases that follow fixes
  rather than waiting for a free weekend.
- **The certificates.** The app is unsigned because an Apple Developer
  membership and an Authenticode certificate are recurring costs with an
  identity attached. They are the first thing sponsorship would buy, and
  the extra click each OS asks for on an unsigned build is what it would
  remove.
- **The machines.** Three operating systems in CI, and the ARM runners the
  release matrix still has to prove.

## What this project can promise

Stated plainly, because the alternative is letting somebody guess:

- **No company behind it and no paid tier.** Nothing is gated, and nothing is
  going to be. A sponsor gets the same app as everybody else.
- **No support contract and no response-time commitment.** Issues are read
  and most get answered; "most" is the honest word.
- **Not funded today.** No foundation, no full-time maintainer. Sponsoring
  is what changes that, and what turns "most issues get answered" into
  "issues get answered".

The MIT licence means the code stays available whatever happens to the
maintainer; it does not mean somebody will be there to fix it. If you are
deciding whether to adopt this somewhere it will be hard to leave, that is
the paragraph to read twice.

## Other ways to help

Not everything is money. A reproducible [bug report](../community/contributing/reporting-a-bug.md), a
[translation](../community/contributing/adding-translations.md), a corrected sentence in a help card,
a star on the [repository](https://github.com/stackvo/stackvo) so that the
next person finds it — each is something the project runs on.
